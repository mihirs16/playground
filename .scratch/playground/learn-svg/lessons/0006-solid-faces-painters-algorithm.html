Rotating SVG · Lesson 6

Solid faces & the painter's algorithm

The win: fill the wireframe cube into a solid, and learn the one rule that decides what's in front of what — plus reproduce and fix the "hollow boxes under rotation" bug from §2 of your concepts doc.

1. A solid is just polygons, painted in the right order

SVG has no depth buffer — it paints shapes in document order, later ones on top. So to fake 3D you sort faces by depth and draw far ones first, near ones last. The near faces paint over the far ones. That's the painter's algorithm — like an artist laying down background before foreground.

faces
  .map(f => ({ f, depth: depthAt(centre(f)) }))   // how far back each face sits
  .sort((a, b) => a.depth - b.depth)               // far → near
  .forEach(({f}) => drawPolygon(f));               // paint in that order

"Depth" here is just (x + y) from the projection — the same term that pushed points "back and down". Bigger x+y = farther away = drawn earlier.

2. The trap: baking in which faces are visible

At a fixed 30° angle, a box only ever shows three faces (top, +x side, +y side). The tempting shortcut is to draw only those three and skip the hidden backs — a static form of back-face culling. It looks perfect... until you rotate.

THE BUG — Once the turntable spins past ~90°, the previously-hidden back faces swing toward you — but they were never drawn. Result: boxes look hollow, like you're seeing through them. The lesson: any visibility shortcut that assumes a fixed camera must be recomputed when the view changes. Culling and depth-sort are view-dependent.

3. See it break, then fix it

Toggle "cull to 3 faces" ON, then drag rotation past 90° — watch the cube go hollow. Turn it OFF (draw all faces, back-to-front) and it stays solid at every angle. The fix costs a couple of overpainted hidden faces; correctness is worth it.

Flat shading (top lightest, sides progressively darker) fakes light with no lighting model — just hand-picked fills, exactly like the prototype's prism().

4. Where this hits the wall (SVG vs three.js)

Sorting whole faces by their centre depth works for separated boxes and a single vertical-axis turntable. It breaks for interpenetrating shapes or free tumbling on a second axis — then you need a real per-pixel z-buffer (three.js/WebGL). That's the exact boundary your concepts doc draws: single-axis spin is fine in SVG; multi-axis, perspective, or lighting → reach for a 3D engine.
