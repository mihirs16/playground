Rotating SVG · Lesson 1

Drawing with coordinates

The win for this lesson: hand-write an SVG that draws a small scene, and understand the coordinate system every later trick (rotation, animation) will build on.

1. An SVG is just a coordinate grid you paint on

An <svg> is a box with its own little coordinate system. The viewBox="minX minY width height" tells the browser what slice of the grid to show. Everything you draw is positioned in those grid units — not pixels.

The one surprise worth burning in now: the Y axis points down. The origin (0,0) is the top-left. Bigger y = lower on screen. (That's exactly why the isometric formula in your prototype writes screenY = … − z — it subtracts height to move things up.)

2. The five primitives you'll actually use

Your prototype is built almost entirely from these. Learn them once:

<rect   x y width height />                 <!-- a box (slab, panel) -->
<circle cx cy r />                          <!-- a dot (tree, node) -->
<line   x1 y1 x2 y2 />                       <!-- one straight segment -->
<polyline points="x1,y1 x2,y2 x3,y3" />     <!-- open path (a route) -->
<polygon  points="x1,y1 x2,y2 x3,y3" />     <!-- closed shape (a face) -->

polygon is the star: every box face in the exploded diagram is one polygon — three or four corner points, auto-closed, filled flat. Master polygon and you can draw any solid.

Two paint attributes appear everywhere: fill (interior colour) and stroke / stroke-width (the outline). A face uses both — a fill to read as a surface, a thin stroke to read as an edge.

3. Try it — edit and watch it redraw

Change numbers below. Move the polygon points. Break it, fix it. This is the whole skill: thinking in grid coordinates.
