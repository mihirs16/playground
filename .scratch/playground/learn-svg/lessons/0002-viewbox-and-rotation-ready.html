Rotating SVG · Lesson 2

The viewBox & building rotation-ready art

The win: control where the origin sits, group a scene so it moves as one, and place your art around a known pivot — the single decision that makes the next lesson (rotation) easy instead of maddening.

1. viewBox = the window onto the grid

viewBox="minX minY width height" picks which rectangle of the infinite coordinate grid is shown, and the browser stretches it to fill the <svg>'s pixel size. Two SVGs can draw the same shape and look totally different purely because of their viewBox.

ROTATION HINT — If your viewBox is "-50 -50 100 100", then (0,0) is the middle of the view. Art built around (0,0) can be rotated with a bare rotate(deg) — no pivot bookkeeping. That's the trick behind clean spins.

2. <g> — group a scene so it moves as one

A <g> is a container. Anything inside it shares the group's transform. Instead of rotating twelve polygons individually, you wrap them in a group and rotate the group once. Your prototype's whole massing is conceptually one group that gets rotated per frame.

<g transform="rotate(20)">
  <polygon … />   <!-- all of these -->
  <rect … />      <!-- rotate together -->
  <circle … />    <!-- as one rigid scene -->
</g>

3. Rotation happens about a pivot

Rotation isn't "spin in place" — it swings every point around one fixed pivot point. Points far from the pivot travel on a big circle; the pivot itself never moves. So the question that decides everything is: where is your pivot?

rotate(deg) pivots about the origin (0,0). rotate(deg cx cy) pivots about (cx,cy). If your art is drawn far from the origin and you use plain rotate(deg), it flings off across the screen — the classic "why did my drawing fly away?" bug.

4. Try it — feel the pivot

The house is a <g>. Change the pivot numbers in rotate(25 50 40) and watch what the drawing swings around. Try rotate(25 0 0) to see it fling from the corner; try the house's own centre.

The orange dot marks the pivot. Notice: when the pivot sits at the house's centre, it spins in place; move the pivot away and the whole house orbits it.
